Prologue

It started with a sentence that had italic words and a trailing space inside the tag.

"Dialogue in straight quotes," she said—and stopped. Then she went on.

1984. This line starts with a number and a period, which pandoc would read as a list.

# This one starts with a hash.

Special characters: an asterisk * in the middle, an_underscore, a dollar $5 and $6, an @handle, a [bracket].

*

A run of text directly inside a div, with a line break
and then a double break

which makes a new paragraph with emphasis across a newline.
* * *

Final paragraph of the prologue with nested bold and italic text and a superscript x2.

Theseus

An epigraph quoting a song by a real author.
Second line of the song.

First paragraph of the second part. It is long enough to compute a length ratio, because the check ignores paragraphs shorter than eighty characters.

Second paragraph, with a list-like opening on a new line
a) like this one,
(1) and this one.

After a horizontal rule. More text to make the part long enough to be split into more than one chunk if the target is small.

Yet another paragraph in the second part, with enough words to push the counter over the small target used in the test.

An invented epigraph opening a chapter of the third part.

Text of the third part, whose title exists only as an image in the original page, so it has to be added by hand from a small table of headings.

A second invented epigraph opening the next chapter, without any heading.

Text of the next chapter, long enough to make the chapter rule cut the chunk here when the target is small enough for this fixture.
